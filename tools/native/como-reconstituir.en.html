Peptides ship lyophilized: a vacuum-dried powder that stays stable for months. Reconstituting simply means putting the water back. It is a simple step, but how you do it decides whether the peptide survives intact or degrades.

What you need

  • Your lyophilized peptide vial, still sealed.
  • Bacteriostatic water, which carries a mild preservative and lets you draw several doses from the same vial. Do not use tap water, bottled purified water, or homemade saline.
  • A sterile syringe to transfer the water.
  • Alcohol wipes for the rubber stoppers of both vials.

How much water to add

There is no universally "correct" amount: the volume you choose changes the concentration, not how much peptide is in the vial. A 10 mg vial holds 10 mg whether you add 1 mL or 3 mL — all that changes is how much liquid each milligram occupies.

The practical approach is to pick a volume that leaves easy numbers to measure. These are the common ones:

A larger volume makes small amounts easier to measure accurately. A smaller volume leaves less liquid to refrigerate. As a rule of thumb we recommend two bacteriostatic water vials per peptide vial, so you have room to work with.

Step by step

  1. Let the vial reach room temperature. If it came out of the fridge, wait a few minutes. Adding water to a very cold vial encourages condensation.
  2. Remove the plastic caps from both vials and wipe both rubber stoppers with an alcohol wipe. Let them air-dry; do not blow on them.
  3. Draw the water. Insert the needle into the bacteriostatic water vial and draw your chosen volume.
  4. Add it slowly, down the side of the vial. Tilt the peptide vial and let the water run down the glass rather than jetting straight onto the powder. A direct stream can damage the peptide's structure.
  5. Do not shake. Ever. Roll the vial gently between your fingers, or simply let it stand. Shaking creates foam and breaks peptide chains.
  6. Wait for it to dissolve completely. This usually takes anywhere from seconds to a couple of minutes. The liquid should end up clear, with no particles or clumps. Copper-containing products such as GHK-Cu, GLOW and KLOW turn a characteristic blue — that is normal.
  7. Label the vial with the reconstitution date and the resulting concentration. It is the most-skipped step and the one you are most grateful for three weeks later.
  8. Refrigerate it straight afterwards.

Signs something is wrong

  • The powder will not dissolve after several minutes standing, or visible particles stay suspended.
  • The liquid is cloudy when it should be clear.
  • An unexpected colour appears. Most peptides give a colourless solution; copper-containing ones are blue. Any other shade is worth asking about before going further.
  • The appearance does not match what that lot's certificate records (white powder versus blue powder, for example).

In any of those cases, do not use the vial — message us with your lot number. You can check the documented appearance for your lot in the certificates section.

After reconstitution

A reconstituted peptide is no longer the stable solid it was: from that point it is kept refrigerated, protected from light, and used within about four weeks. The details are in storage and shelf life.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use regular water or saline?

For a vial you will draw from more than once, no. Bacteriostatic water contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol, which prevents bacterial growth inside the vial once the stopper has been punctured. Preservative-free sterile water and saline offer no such protection, and tap or bottled water is not sterile at all. The full comparison is in bacteriostatic water vs sterile water.

Does adding more water change the potency?

No. The amount of peptide in the vial is the same; what changes is the concentration — how much liquid each milligram sits in. Adding more water dilutes it, it does not reduce or increase the total content.

Why can't I shake it?

Peptides are relatively fragile chains of amino acids. Vigorous shaking introduces air, creates foam, and subjects the chains to forces that can break or denature them. Gently rolling the vial, or simply letting it stand, dissolves the powder just as well without that risk.

How long does it take to dissolve?

Usually seconds to a couple of minutes. If particles are still visible after five minutes of standing, do not force it by shaking — message us with your lot number.
