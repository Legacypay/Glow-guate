A lyophilized peptide is remarkably stable: dry, sealed and cold, it keeps for months without losing quality. Reconstituted is a different matter. The general rule is simple — water is what starts degradation, so the peptide lasts as long as it stays dry, cold and dark.

Before reconstitution

The lyophilized powder is the most stable form your peptide will ever be in. Keep it refrigerated if you will use it within the next few weeks, or frozen if you are storing it longer. Keep the vial sealed and in its box: clear glass offers no protection from light.

Two things to avoid. The first is repeated freeze-thaw cycling — taking a vial out of the freezer, letting it reach room temperature and refreezing it several times stresses the material. The second is moisture; if you are opening a vial that was cold, let it reach room temperature before uncapping so water does not condense inside.

After reconstitution

The moment you add water, the clock starts. From then on:

  • Always refrigerated, between 2 and 8 °C. The fridge door swings most in temperature; an inner shelf is better.
  • Protected from light. Keep it in its box or an opaque bag.
  • Used within about four weeks. That is the standard reference, and it lines up with the working life of bacteriostatic water's preservative.
  • Not frozen. Freezing a reconstituted solution forms ice crystals that damage peptide chains. The freezer is for powder, not liquid.
  • Labelled with the reconstitution date and the concentration. Without it, by week three you are guessing.

Transport and climate

In Guatemala heat is the factor working hardest against you, especially on the coast and in the lowlands. If you collect your order or receive it at home, get it into the fridge as soon as you can and do not leave it in a vehicle in the sun: the inside of a parked car passes 50 °C easily.

For journeys over an hour, a small cooler with one gel pack is enough. Do not let the vial touch the frozen pack directly — wrap it in a cloth so it stays cold without freezing.

How to tell a vial is done

  • The liquid, previously clear, has turned cloudy or has particles suspended in it.
  • Flakes, strands or sediment have appeared at the bottom.
  • The colour has changed from when you reconstituted it.
  • Considerably more than four weeks have passed since reconstitution.
  • The vial spent a long stretch out of the fridge, or froze by accident.

In any of those cases, discard it. A degraded peptide does not always look different — but when it does look different, there is no doubt left.

Frequently asked questions

How long does an unopened vial last?

A sealed lyophilized vial kept cold and away from light stays stable for months. The reference date for your lot is in its documentation; you can look up your lot's certificate in the certificates section.

What if it sat out of the fridge for a few hours?

A sealed lyophilized vial tolerates a few hours at room temperature well — it travels that way. A reconstituted vial is more sensitive: a few hours somewhere cool is usually not a problem, but a full day in the heat is. If in doubt, check the appearance before using it.

Can I freeze a reconstituted vial to make it last longer?

Not advisable. Ice crystal formation damages peptide chains, and the deterioration is not always visible. If you need to store material for a long time, keep it lyophilized and only reconstitute what you will use in the coming month.

Why four weeks?

It is the conventional reference for a peptide reconstituted with bacteriostatic water and kept refrigerated: it lines up roughly with the preservative's working life once the vial has been punctured, and with the window in which most peptides stay stable in solution.
