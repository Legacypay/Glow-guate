What the benzyl alcohol does

"Bacteriostatic" does not mean it kills bacteria — it means it stops them multiplying. Benzyl alcohol at 0.9% keeps the vial's contents inhospitable to bacterial growth, which is exactly what is needed when the stopper will be punctured more than once.

Every puncture is an opportunity for entry. However careful the technique — wiping the stopper, a fresh needle each time — a vial opened repeatedly and kept for weeks accumulates risk. The preservative is what makes that repeated use reasonable.

Why sterile water alone is not enough for a multi-dose vial

Sterile water is perfectly sterile inside the sealed container. The moment the stopper is punctured, that sterility is no longer guaranteed: nothing inside the vial prevents any organism that got in from multiplying, and a preservative-free liquid kept in the fridge for days is as good a growth medium as any.

That is why sterile water comes in single-use ampoules and vials: it is meant to be opened, used in full and discarded. If you use it to reconstitute a peptide you will draw from over several weeks, the peptide may stay intact but the liquid stops being reliable.

Which to use

For essentially every lyophilized peptide we sell, the answer is bacteriostatic water. The vials hold several doses by design and are used over weeks, which is precisely the scenario the preservative exists for.

Sterile water makes sense in two specific situations: when the entire contents will be used at once, and when benzyl alcohol is unwanted because of a known sensitivity. Outside those cases, bacteriostatic water is the practical choice.

What should never be used: tap water, bottled water, pharmacy purified water without a sterile designation, or home-prepared solutions. None are sterile, and none are suitable for reconstitution.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a bacteriostatic water vial last once opened?

The preservative keeps the contents protected through weeks of normal use. The usual reference is 28 days from first puncture, kept somewhere cool and clean. Write the opening date on the label.

Does benzyl alcohol affect the peptide?

At the 0.9% concentration used in bacteriostatic water it is the standard solvent for reconstituting lyophilized peptides and is compatible with them. That is why it is the presentation conventionally paired with multi-dose vials.

Can I mix the two?

There is no reason to. Diluting bacteriostatic water with sterile water lowers the preservative concentration and with it the protection it provides. Use one or the other as the situation calls for.

Does bacteriostatic water need refrigeration?

Before opening, no — it keeps at room temperature, away from light. Once punctured, storing it somewhere cool and clean extends its usable life.
